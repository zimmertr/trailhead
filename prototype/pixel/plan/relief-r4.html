Trailhead Map Layouts

You said the mountains sat on the horizon like the back board of a puppet show, and that they should be part of the world. Here are three ways to do that, each drawn as a rough block-in. In all three the land now has real height, so a slope in front hides what is behind it. Nothing here is final art.

Layout Show

The Forest Fog, First Try

The fog now lies inside the rainforest, as in your picture, and the sea and beach are clear. It starts from nothing beside the lake and builds westward. The forest has low ridges, the fog fills the hollows between them, and each ridge carries a rank of trees that is paler than the one in front.

What is wrong with it so far: the trees fade to pale green where your picture fades to grey, the fog pools read a little like snow, and it is not spooky yet (no bare snags against white, no eyes at Dusk). It is the same in all three layouts.

Rough in All Three

  • Mount Monolith is a stand-in cone with stripes. Its shape was better on the first block-in and will come back.
  • The ranges are rows of similar lumps, and the granite is too pale. The two green spurs beside the glacier are plain cones.
  • The dry side is one speckled slope. The far ranges are flat cut-outs.
  • Water now gathers: the meltwater and the stream out of the tarns join, and the river is wider below each joining. The chain of tarns is drawn but small.
  • Not in: animals, signs, bridges, clouds, Dusk.
